# Method repertoire (criterion C5)

The anchor paper draws EI's methods from Information Systems (requirements engineering, systems architecture, evaluation design) and from educational research, and claims **specificity, not novelty** [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]. This page lists the repertoire, marking for each method whether it is **available** as is or **needs adaptation** for education. The adaptations are where EI's methodological specificity would come from.

| Method | Origin | What it contributes to EI | Status | Adaptation needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design-oriented research, reference modelling | IS / Business Informatics [@Osterle2011Memorandum; @Helfert2008Business] | Builds and evaluates artefacts (models, architectures, governance mechanisms) | Available | Pedagogical criterion as an evaluation criterion; governance responsibility |
| Requirements engineering with educators, learners and administrators | IS | Translates educational aims into specifications | Available | Contested aims must be represented, not resolved away [@Biesta2007Works] |
| Evidence-centred design, argument-based validation | Educational measurement [@Mislevy2003Structure; @Kane2013Validating] | Explicit inference chains from observation to claim | Available | Extend from assessment to the whole decision lifecycle and to institutional decisions |
| Data-based decision cycles | School improvement, special education [@Schildkamp2019Data; @NCII2013Databased; @Hamilton2009Using] | Goal–evidence–action–evaluation loops | Available as practice | Computable shared model; evidence base is weak |
| Decision-value testing | Own proposal ([uniqueness argument](../discipline/uniqueness-argument.md)) | Does an evidence class change a defensible decision beyond cheaper evidence? | **To develop** | Core EI method candidate |
| Micro-randomised trials | Mobile health [@Klasnja2015Microrandomized] | Effects of options at decision points | Needs adaptation | Cluster structure (teachers act on classes); only among options judged equivalent |
| Federated network studies on a common data model | Medical informatics [@Hripcsak2015Observational] | Replication across sites without pooling personal data | Needs adaptation | An educational common model; data trusts as governance [@Wieczorek2026Beyond] |
| Semantic construct mapping | Psychology [@Wulff2025Semantic] | Detects jingle-jangle across measures before pooling | Needs testing | Transfer to educational constructs is untested |
| Calibration with known results | Methodological practice | Negative control (learning styles) and positive control (expertise reversal) | Available | [@Pashler2008Learning; @Tetzlaff2025Cornerstone] |
| Explanatory learner modelling | LA/EDM [@Rose2019Explanatory] | Interpretable models of learning | Available | Link to decisions and expiry |
| Programme-convergence document analysis | Learning Sciences [@Sommerhoff2018Teach] | Tests criterion C6 empirically | Available | Apply to independently developed EI programmes |
| Ethnographic and infrastructural analysis | Critical data studies, infrastructure studies [@Hartong2019Opening; @Wieczorek2026Beyond; @Chen2024Framework] | Shows how data infrastructures act on the governed | Available | Pair with a constructive counterpart (the anchor paper's main gap) |
| Qualitative study of instructional decision-making | LA [@Wise2019Teaching] | How educators actually use data | Available | Across levels and sectors |

## The argument this table must support

Methods are drawn from neighbours, and that is acceptable. What has to be shown is that **the combination is required by the object and is not just assembled**. Three adaptations carry most of that weight: decision-value testing, decision-point experimentation for collective settings, and expiry-aware federated replication. If none of them proves workable in use, C5 stays at "partly satisfied".
