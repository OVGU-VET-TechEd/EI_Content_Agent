# Lifelong learner model ("the learner as a walking data model")

## The idea

The individual lifelong learner as a "walking data model" with an education focus. It would draw on datasets and standardised systems from psychology, neuroscience, educational and social science, with a base model covering all learning parameters across fields (user's notes, `Thoughts_AI_Medicine.md`).

## Prior art

Kay proposed lifelong learner models that learners own and control, reused across applications and life stages [@Kay2008Lifelong]. The originality claim therefore cannot rest on the idea itself.

## Case for (at full strength)

- The learner is the only constant across school, VET, university and work.
- A learner-owned model fits sovereignty and portability, and directly addresses fragmentation across institutions.
- It gives the field a memorable picture.
- Portfolios already show that learner-curated, longitudinal evidence is feasible ([learner portfolios](../approaches/learner-portfolios.md)).

## Weaknesses

- Not original (Kay).
- Its most visible data (attention, 24-hour context) lie closest to prohibited or contested practice ([AI Act](../governance/ai-act-and-learner-data.md)).
- It conflicts with the critical data studies the anchor paper relies on: data-derived norms narrow learners' conduct [@Hautz2026Algorithmic], and monitoring data diverge from what they represent [@Hartong2019Opening].
- "All parameters across the fields" is unbounded and hard to falsify.
- It is unsafe with minors.

## Rescue

Make it a **learner-held view computed from consented [decision records](educational-decision-record.md)**: open by default, with retention limits, and no continuous biometrics. This keeps the attractive part (ownership, portability) without making it the field's foundation. See the open decision in [open questions](../discipline/open-questions.md).
