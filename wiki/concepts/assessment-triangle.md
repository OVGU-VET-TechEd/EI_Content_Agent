# Assessment triangle and evidence-centred inference

## The triangle

*Knowing What Students Know* frames assessment as reasoning from evidence through three elements that must be designed **together** [@NRC2001Knowing]:

| Element | Question | Implication for data |
|---|---|---|
| Cognition | What does competence in this domain consist of, and how does it develop? | Specify the knowledge, strategies and reasoning of interest, not only an overall score |
| Observation | Which tasks or situations make that competence visible? | Collect work, explanations and performances that reveal thinking |
| Interpretation | What can we reasonably infer from those observations? | Establish how evidence distinguishes levels of understanding or kinds of difficulty |

The report also holds that no single assessment serves every purpose equally well, and that performance cannot be read fairly without the learner's **opportunity to learn** (instructional history).

## Formal extensions

- **Evidence-centred design** structures the assessment argument as student, evidence and task models [@Mislevy2003Structure].
- **Argument-based validation** makes the interpretation/use argument explicit and tests its weakest inferences [@Kane2013Validating].

## Why this matters for EI

These are the most rigorous accounts of *educational inference*, but they centre on assessment. The EI move ([uniqueness argument](../discipline/uniqueness-argument.md)) extends the inference chain to the **whole decision lifecycle**: from aim and options, through action and response, to expiry, at classroom, institutional and system level. Measurement specialists will rightly ask what is new. The answer must be the decision and governance layer, not the inference logic itself.

## A basic decision-specific evidence set (own synthesis, not a validated instrument)

| Domain | Question | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Learning goal | What should the learner become able to do? | Defined competence, success criteria |
| Current competence | What can they do independently now? | Short diagnostic task, authentic performance |
| Reasoning and difficulty | What explains the performance? | Explanation, error patterns, work samples |
| Instructional history | What teaching and practice have they had? | Topics covered, practice, prior feedback |
| Learning conditions | Do task and setting enable the learner? | Access, tools, distractions, support |
| Response over time | Which support helps, and how much? | Repeated comparable performances linked to documented changes |
