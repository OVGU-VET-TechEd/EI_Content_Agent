# Interface discipline

**Definition (stipulated):** a field constituted by translating between two established domains rather than by a method of its own. The **interface profession** performs that translation inside institutions [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

## Reference cases

| Field | Characterisation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Business Informatics (Wirtschaftsinformatik) | An engineering-oriented perspective on information systems, integrating management and computing to design and improve organisational systems | [@Helfert2008Business] |
| Design-oriented IS research | The German-speaking tradition behind Business Informatics: rigour and relevance through designing artefacts | [@Osterle2011Memorandum] |
| Engineering Informatics | Integrates IT with engineering disciplines; advanced ICT applied to engineering processes | [@Xu2014Engineering] |
| Medical / health informatics | Shared data models and network studies; Educational Informatics teams now proposed inside medical education | [@Hripcsak2015Observational; @Turner2024Takes] |

The anchor paper's alignment table (Appendix A.1) maps each field's domain, key problem, data domain and professional output. EI's key data domain is learner records, assessment data, interaction logs and longitudinal cohort data. Its outputs are LMS/SIS governance, AI deployment policies, data-stewardship frameworks and procurement specifications [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

## The limit of the analogy

"A domain pairing establishes a programme, not a field" [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]. Analogy can make a proposal plausible. It cannot warrant it. What warrants a field is a specified object, an adequate theoretical repertoire, a methodological toolkit and a defended boundary.

## Pattern behind the reference cases (own synthesis)

Each interface field owns (a) a **constitutive artefact**, a formal model of what its domain acts on, and (b) a **validity test** for that artefact that the domain imposes. Business Informatics: reference models, tested by whether the system works in the organisation. Engineering Informatics: physically faithful models, tested by prediction within tolerance. Medical informatics: comparable records, tested by replication across sites. The [uniqueness argument](../discipline/uniqueness-argument.md) proposes EI's equivalent. This pattern needs its own literature check.
