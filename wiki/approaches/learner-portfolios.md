# Learner portfolios (UK practice)

## Definition

In UK higher education a portfolio is a **structured collection of work demonstrating how a student has met module learning outcomes**. It may include written work, reflective commentary, creative and practice-based outputs, and records of professional learning. It works best when the components form a coherent learning narrative rather than a container of unrelated tasks [@SussexPortfolio; @EdinburghPortfolios].

Working definition for research (user's notes): *a purposefully structured, longitudinal collection of learner- and teacher-generated evidence, with reflection and feedback, used to document, interpret and support learning and to inform subsequent teaching decisions.*

## Design as an evidence system (Warwick outline)

Warwick's assessment-design toolkit treats the portfolio as a **designed evidence-gathering system** [@WarwickPortfolios]:

1. Identify the curriculum outcomes to address, including reflective or meta-learning outcomes.
2. **Decide which decisions the portfolio will inform**: formative feedback, component grades, an overall grade, or a mix.
3. Design constructively aligned tasks.
4. Define criteria and standards for tasks and for the whole portfolio.
5. Plan formative checkpoints, feedback and feed-forward.
6. Decide who assesses: self, peers, staff.

Step 2 is the decision-anchored logic EI generalises ([decision record](../concepts/educational-decision-record.md)).

## Types

| Type | Purpose | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Learning portfolio | Document the learning process | Drafts, reflections, feedback, goals |
| Assessment portfolio | Demonstrate achievement against outcomes | Final artefacts, rubric-aligned commentary |
| Professional / teaching portfolio | Evidence professional competence | Lesson plans, observations, CPD records |
| Showcase portfolio | Present selected best work | Curated artefacts |
| ePortfolio | Collect, reflect and share digitally | Multimedia artefacts, comments, version histories |

## Data a portfolio generates

Performance data (artefacts); process data (drafts, revisions, logs); diagnostic data (misconceptions, gaps); feedback data (teacher, peer and self, plus the learner's response); contextual data (goals, conditions, placements); longitudinal data (repeated samples over time). Process portfolios in design and technology capture learners' designing as well as products [@Stables2017Use].

## From evidence to teaching: the cycle

**Collect → make thinking visible → interpret against criteria → give dialogic feedback → adapt teaching → track impact.** This mirrors formative assessment [@Black2009Developing]. Embedding formative assessment at scale has been tested in a 140-school English trial [@Anders2022Effect]. It also fits evidence-informed teaching in England, which combines research, school data, professional experience and evaluation of practice changes [@Coldwell2017Evidence].

## Design principles for research

- Align evidence with constructs; do not treat "everything in the portfolio" as data.
- Use structured reflection prompts ("What evidence shows progress?", "What teaching action followed?").
- Sample longitudinally: baseline, midpoint, endpoint.
- Triangulate with observation, assessment scores or analytics.
- Protect authenticity without letting selection become presentation.
- Plan workload and calibrate markers: inter-rater reliability is low without shared rubrics [@EdinburghPortfolios].

## Characteristics on the [data-based grid](data-based-approaches.md)

| Characteristic | Portfolios |
|---|---|
| Decision anchor | Explicit when designed well (Warwick step 2) |
| Unit | Individual learner over time |
| Evidence classes | Artefacts, reflections, feedback: rich, low-tech, consent-friendly |
| Actor and agency | **High learner agency**: learner selects and explains evidence |
| Validity approach | Criteria and rubrics; reliability is the weak point |
| Expiry | Not specified. Showcase portfolios persist, learning portfolios age |
| Governance | ePortfolio platforms raise portability, retention and ownership questions |

## EI relevance

- A working, low-surveillance model of **learner-held longitudinal evidence**. This is the defensible core of the [lifelong learner model](../concepts/lifelong-learner-model.md) idea.
- Shows that **the decision to be informed** can be designed in up front, as in DBI and the decision record.
- Open EI questions: portability of ePortfolio evidence across institutions (interoperability, learner records); who stewards the data; when evidence should expire.
