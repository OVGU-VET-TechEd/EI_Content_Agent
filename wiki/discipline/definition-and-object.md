# Definition, object and terms

## Working definition

Education Informatics (EI; German: *Bildungsinformatik*) is proposed as an **interface discipline that engineers socio-technical educational systems under educational constraints and public-interest objectives**. It translates educational purposes and constraints into implementable architectures, data structures, governance mechanisms and evaluation designs [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

The claim is **constitutive, not descriptive**: the term circulates, but it has no stable referent, no shared definitional core, no citation community and no journal home [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]. Earlier proposals used the name differently. Collins and Weiner proposed a subdiscipline about discovering, organising and communicating educational information, and the same special issue discussed its policy implications [@Collins2010Proposal; @Carr2010Policy; @Carr2010Introduction]. Neighbouring labels are compared in [rival fields](rival-fields.md).

## Object of inquiry

The **educational socio-technical system**: the architecture, data and governance arrangements through which education is delivered, together with the roles, work practices and institutional rules that operate them. The object is "neither the technology nor the pedagogy but the arrangement that binds them" [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

## Stipulated terms

These definitions are stipulative. They fix the terms on which the argument is evaluated; they do not report settled usage [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]. Their uptake by other authors is the test for criterion 4 (see [criteria](disciplinarity-criteria.md)).

| Term | Meaning in EI |
|---|---|
| Educational information system | An information system whose function is constitutive of educational provision or its administration (LMS, student information systems, assessment platforms, credentialing infrastructures, and the data flowing between them), as distinct from generic institutional IT |
| Data stewardship | Allocated, accountable responsibility for educational data across its life cycle: collection, classification, retention, access, portability, deletion. A role-bearing term: someone stewards the data, and in education that someone is often unspecified |
| AI governance | Institutional arrangements (approval, risk assessment, human oversight, contestation, monitoring, audit) under which AI functions enter educational processes and remain answerable. Distinct from AI ethics (principles) and AI safety (model behaviour) |
| Digital sovereignty | The gradual, institutional capacity of a public education system to determine, inspect and change the terms on which its digital infrastructure operates. A capability, not ownership; not the same as national self-hosting |
| Interface discipline / interface profession | A field constituted by translating between two established domains rather than by a method of its own; the professional role that performs this translation inside institutions |
| Informatics in education / informatics education | The broad activity of applying computing to educational ends; versus teaching computing as a subject. EI is about the former |

Public ownership alone does not secure sovereignty. Mein Bildungsraum reproduced narrow models of learning despite public funding and strong data protection [@Richter2025Digital]. This is why sovereignty is defined as a capability.

## What EI is not

- Not *informatics education* (teaching computing as a subject).
- Not educational administration alone: it requires technical and data competence.
- Not generic IT services: it requires pedagogical reasoning and ethical–institutional design competence.
- Not a demand that teachers design the technology themselves: it enables teachers and institutions to use technology well through maintainable, governed systems [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].
- Not technology-first: EI answers to a **pedagogical criterion**, meaning technologies are designed from an account of learning, not of the technology [@Luckin2019Designing]. It takes its object and critical vocabulary from the diagnostic traditions and its methods from design-oriented IS [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

## Competency profile (field level)

EI prepares professionals to [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]:

1. Translate educational requirements into technical specifications and interoperability-aware architectures.
2. Evaluate digital interventions in context, integrating evidence with educational values and feasibility constraints.
3. Design data governance (privacy, access, accountability) consistent with public-interest and human-rights frameworks.
4. Build competency development pathways aligned with ICT-CFT, AI competency frameworks and AI-TPACK [@Mishra2006Technological].
5. Support all sectors (school, VET, higher education, lifelong learning) through domain-specific system design and governance.

The profile describes the scope of a field, not one person's head. The unallocated function is **integration**: holding requirement, architecture, data regime, evaluation design and pedagogical criterion in a single view long enough to reach a defensible decision, and remaining answerable for it. EI trains the integrative member of a multidisciplinary team. If existing team compositions already discharge that function, the professional case fails [@Tegelbeckers2026Education].

## Three institutionalisation pathways

| Pathway | Actors | Artefacts | Feasibility condition | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Academic | Universities, funders, journals | Degree programmes, doctoral training, a venue, a research agenda | Independently developed programmes converge in object and method | Learning Sciences programme analysis [@Sommerhoff2018Teach] |
| Professional | Associations, ministries, employers | Role definitions, competency standards, certification | The work already exists and is done informally | Educational Informatics teams in medical education [@Turner2024Takes] |
| Governance | Ministries, regulators, procurement bodies | Procurement standards, DPIA and equity assessments, AI approval and audit | Obligations already exist (GDPR, AI Act) but capacity does not | AI Act high-risk duties for education ([AI Act page](../governance/ai-act-and-learner-data.md)) |

The anchor paper argues that the governance pathway is already obligatory and the academic pathway should come **last** [@Tegelbeckers2026Education]. Proposals built on this wiki should not contradict that without saying so.
